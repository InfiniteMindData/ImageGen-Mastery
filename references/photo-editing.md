# Photo edits and visual diagnosis

Start with the source at full frame. Identify where the subject meets the scene. Hair against a wall, a sleeve against a hand, or a shoe on the floor often reveals a bad edit earlier than the center of the edited area. Inspect again at the intended display size. Preserve deliberate grain, motion, illustration marks or soft focus when they belong to the image.

## Portrait realism

Look at skin across lit, shadowed and out-of-focus areas. Fine texture should follow focus and illumination. If the forehead looks waxy while the brows resolve clearly, restore subtle local texture on the focused plane. Adding sharp pores everywhere also roughens blurred cheeks. Check that catchlights and facial highlights agree with the apparent light sources. Preserve asymmetry, expression and facial geometry through relighting.

Read hair in masses first, then check the silhouette for loose strands. If every strand is equally sharp, soften the receding hair while keeping the near contour legible. For clothing, follow the load from shoulder seams to hanging fabric. Folds bunch near a bent elbow and stretch along tension lines. A collar casting a shadow should receive light consistent with the neck. Keep seams and closures attached to the garment when the wearer turns.

For a pose, trace the line of support from a planted foot through the hips, or from a seated body to the chair. Count only visible fingers and distinguish occlusion from a missing digit. If a mug appears suspended, repair the finger overlap and small contact shadow before changing the entire arm.

## Match an edit to the cause

| Change | Inspect in source | Direction and acceptance check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warmer light | Compare surfaces facing the key light with those in ambient shadow. Use familiar neutral surfaces when available. | Warm the lit surfaces consistently. A neutral wall in shade can remain cooler. Check that skin highlights retain detail, shadows still have separation, and unrelated colors survive. |
| Replace jacket | Note shoulder slope, elbow bend, wrist position and occluding hair or bag straps. | Limit the edit to the garment plus affected edge shadows. Follow the pose and original fabric tension. Verify the hands, straps and face against the baseline. |
| Replace background | Locate eye level, floor contact, vanishing directions, light direction and focus falloff. | Match the new ground plane to the feet. Add the appropriate contact shadow and scene spill. Check edges on both light and dark surroundings. A blanket blur cannot reconcile incompatible camera heights. |
| Reframe a shot | Mark subject extremities, gaze direction and negative space needed for use. | Prefer a crop when it retains the required content. Extend the canvas only where extra scene information is necessary. Check edges for clipped fingers, repeated props and a shifted horizon. |
| Fix a reflective object | Identify visible environment shapes that could produce a bright stripe or reflection. | Move or soften the reflected shape consistently with surface curvature. On rough metal the highlight spreads; on polished metal it can remain legible. Check nearby objects under the same source. |
| Naturalize a processed photo | Compare contours for halos and flat areas for smeared noise. Determine whether the requested problem is exposure, color, texture or geometry. | Remove the specific defect at its source. If no additional detail exists, preserve the soft area. Record synthesized detail as synthesized. |

## Bound the edit

Make a small map in words: edit area, affected surroundings, protected regions. A new lamp can affect a wall beyond the lamp's outline. A clothing edit can change a shadow under the sleeve while the hand itself remains locked. Ask before relaxing an exact lock if the requested effect requires it. Retain the original and an accepted version before any destructive operation.

**Example.** “Move this studio portrait to a beach at dusk.” If the original shows soft light from image left, a background with broad open sky on that side can preserve the face lighting. If the requested sunset must be on the opposite side, relighting the subject is a separate change. Explain the conflict if the user has locked the subject pixels.

In stylized work, evaluate consistency with the selected rendering rule. A two-tone cel-shaded face may correctly have a hard cheek shadow and no pores. Judge shadow placement and shape in that style.
